Uzbekistan in 7 days: how to get there, what it costs, what you need

Nonstop from New York, visa-free for U.S. citizens since January 2026, fast trains between the Silk Road cities — and a full week for about $1,650.

What you need to know

EntryU.S. citizens can visit visa-free for up to 30 days (since 1 January 2026). Your hotel registers your stay. Always check the latest rules before you fly.
Getting thereNonstop from New York (JFK) to Tashkent with Uzbekistan Airways (Boeing 787), or one stop via Istanbul or Dubai.
Getting aroundFast trains: Tashkent → Samarkand about 2 hours, Samarkand → Bukhara about 1.5 hours, Bukhara → Khiva about 6 hours. Tickets on the national railway website open 30–45 days ahead.
Budget (our estimate)Round-trip flight from the U.S. East Coast around $1,100 plus about $80 a day on the ground (mid-range) — roughly $1,650 for 7 days. Prices vary by season; check current fares.

A 7-day route

  1. Days 1–2 · Tashkent — arrive, bazaar, metro, old town.
  2. Days 3–4 · Samarkand — fast train; Registan, Shah-i-Zinda, Gur-e-Amir.
  3. Days 5–6 · Bukhara — old town on foot, bazaars, evening by the Lyab-i-Hauz pond.
  4. Day 7 · Khiva — the walled city at sunset; fly back via Urgench or Tashkent.

Four cities, one Silk Road

Samarkand

The Registan square and the blue-tiled street of tombs at Shah-i-Zinda. A UNESCO World Heritage city that traded with China and Persia for two thousand years.

Bukhara

A walkable old town of domed bazaars, madrasas and the 12th-century Kalyan minaret. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Khiva

Itchan Kala, a complete walled inner city of mud-brick walls and minarets — best at sunset from the city wall.

Tashkent

The modern capital: wide avenues, the Chorsu bazaar and metro stations decorated like palaces. Your arrival point.

Why travellers come back

Food

Plov cooked in huge cauldrons, fresh bread from clay ovens, melons in summer. Eating out is part of the trip, not a cost line.

Safety

The U.S. State Department rates Uzbekistan Level 1 — exercise normal precautions, the lowest of its four levels (January 2026).

Hospitality

Small family guesthouses in the old towns, tea offered by strangers, and very few crowds outside the main squares.
